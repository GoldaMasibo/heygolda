01 Who I am

A reader, a critical thinker, a People and Culture professional.

Human beings are naturally biased. Try to make their biases work for you.

Golda Masibo, on getting hired

I believe good work should be fair, clear and kind. I care about the people on the other side of the hiring table, and I build the processes that make those values tangible.

I'm a People and Culture professional at heart, who happens to spend most of her days recruiting.

  • Talent acquisition
  • Automation
  • Learning German

I'm interested in the entire journey a person takes into an organisation, from the first job advert and initial conversation to the interview, the offer, and ultimately, how they feel about the experience.

I'm happiest when a messy process becomes a clear one. And when I catch myself doing something manually for the third time, I start looking for a way to automate it.

Outside work, I read, walk and lift weights. I also regularly try to convince my friends to move to my side of town so we can do all three together.

One day, I'd love to run a little mobile coffee shop with my sister, a qualified pastry chef. I'm a sucker for a pretty coffee stand, and I think the two of us could make something lovely.

02 Work

What I do well

Processes I've made better, and people I've helped find the right seat.

  • 1

    Referral programmes people actually use

    I took a staff referral programme from an idea people liked to a structured process that employees could easily follow and I could track. The goal was to make participation simple and accountability possible.

  • 2

    Calendars that book themselves

    I built an automated scheduling process that allows senior leaders to book their own interviews, eliminating the endless email exchanges that used to come with finding a suitable time.

  • 3

    Hires who stay

    Every person I've hired so far has passed probation, and none have left yet. I'm proud of that. Getting someone through the door matters, but so does finding someone who wants to stay.

  • 4

    Understanding a culture before hiring into it

    I take time to understand how a team operates, what it values and what makes someone successful in that environment. I don't just look for someone who can do the job. I look for someone who can thrive in the context they'll be doing it in.

04 Builds

Things I've made

Because I got tired of doing things by hand.

Automation

The candidate journey

Google Apps Script, Forms and Calendar working together to make parts of recruitment run themselves. Candidates book their own interviews, assignments are sent automatically, and fewer things get lost in the back-and-forth.

Craft

The application kit

A collection of CVs, notes and outreach materials I've developed with Claude and refined until they sound like me. A little AI assistance, a lot of editing, and absolutely no tolerance for generic corporate language.

An idea I'm still chasing

Kora

Recruitment and people data live in too many places. Kora is my idea for bringing that information together and turning it into clear, useful narratives that People teams can actually act on.

Where it stands: I have a spreadsheet prototype, a plan to test the idea with a handful of HR managers and a roadmap for what comes next. The part I'm still working through is moving from a spreadsheet prototype to a properly built product in Python.

The more time I spend in People and Culture, the more I see Kora becoming something bigger than a tool for centralising HR data. I'm still figuring out what that looks like.

And, selfishly, I hope nobody builds it before I do.

09 Notes

For job seekers

A few years of hiring, and a lot of CVs.

  1. Do not vibe apply

    Skip roles that are not a good fit for you. Your time is better spent on fewer, better applications.

  2. One role per company, not ten

    You may love what they love, but applying for a finance internship and a VP of People and Culture in the same week is a red flag.

  3. People are biased, so make it work for you

    A clean, readable CV, preferably a PDF. Show up to interviews looking like you like yourself: good internet if virtual, a put-together look, clear speech. And for goodness' sake, be brief.

Faith, agency and what I'm unlearning

I'm a Pentecostal Christian. My faith matters to me, but I've also been thinking about the ways organised religion can sometimes discourage personal agency, particularly when we're taught to surrender decisions, ambitions and responsibilities that are ours to carry.

I'm exploring what it means to take ownership of my life without losing my relationship with God. I don't want to abandon my faith. I want to examine the things I've been taught, question the ones that no longer serve me and hold on to what remains meaningful. Believing in something higher also protects my sanity.

I still believe in God. I just don't think belief and personal agency have to compete.
