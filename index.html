About

The short version, in my own voice.

I work in talent acquisition at a pan-African university, where I look after hiring from the first brief to the first day. I like the whole journey: the job ad, the screen, the interview, the offer, and the part nobody thinks about, which is how it all felt for the person on the other side.

I am happiest when a messy process turns into a clear one. If I catch myself doing the same thing for the third time, I start writing a script. Forms, calendars, trackers and handover emails have all lost their manual parts that way.

Outside work I read, walk, lift, and nag my friends to move to my side of town so we can do all three together. I also help with a small online accessories and footwear business that belongs to my sister.

Notes

What a few years of hiring and tens of thousands of CVs taught me.

  1. Do not vibe apply

    Skip roles that are not a good fit. Your time is better spent on fewer, better applications.

  2. One role per company, not ten

    You may love what they love, but applying for a finance internship and a VP of People and Culture in the same week is a red flag.

  3. People are biased, so make it work for you

    A clean, readable CV, preferably a PDF. Show up to interviews looking like you like yourself: good internet if virtual, a put-together look, clear speech. And for goodness' sake, be brief.
